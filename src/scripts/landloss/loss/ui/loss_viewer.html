Scenario to compare with the baseline

Design a policy here. Nothing is valued: each claim is paid the lesser of its repair cost and its cap, less the excess. With sub-caps on, the repair of retaining walls and of bridges and culverts is each limited by its own sub-cap, and the rest of the repair by the land cap; with them off, the land cap limits the whole repair. Every limit is per dwelling. Every figure, chart and the map can show the scenario beside the baseline (the NHI Act 2023 as set on the left). All amounts exclude GST.

Damage

Costs

Fixed on every claim with wall works, on top of the walls as built (site and specification allowances and the wall rate scaling all in), each on the one before:
Professional fees (20% of the walls, at least $5,100)
Mob/demob (higher of $5,000 or 15% of the walls and professional fees)
Project management (10% of the walls, professional fees, mob/demob and any culvert or bridge — so on a claim with only a crossing too)
And on every claim, a contingency (20% of the whole repair cost — liquefaction and culverts and bridges included — applied last)

Sensitivity

The Canterbury liquefaction costs are in 2010/2011 dollars. 1.42 is a placeholder: the growth in construction labour costs from the March 2011 quarter (1,030) to the December 2025 quarter (1,465) in the Stats NZ Labour Cost Index (salary and ordinary time wage rates, construction, series LCIQ.SG51E9). It follows pay rates, not plant or materials.
Yellow fields are the baseline's policy settings, and purple fields at the top the scenario's; blue fields scale the model's own estimates, to see how far an answer leans on them. The wall rates set both what a wall costs to replace and what it was worth, since both come from the same rate. Which properties are insured is not known, so that share of them, chosen at random but fixed per property, is insured; the rest are paid nothing and are not claims. Every figure excludes GST, including the excess and land caps typed in. Everything else in the CSV is fixed by exposure and vulnerability.

Map viewer: where the claims are

These map settings only change how the map is drawn — which dots show, how they are coloured, the basemap. They do not change any figure or chart: the settlements, totals and charts follow the baseline settings on the left and the scenario at the top. The Baseline / Scenario switch picks whose settlement, cap and basis the dots are coloured and shown by.

Each dot is a property; click one to see how it settled. The basemap is fetched from a tile server, so it needs internet — choose None to work entirely offline. No claim data ever leaves this page.
