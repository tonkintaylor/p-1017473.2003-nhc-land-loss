The retaining wall reviewer page now defines a pip (potential instability point) and a pif (potential instability face) in full in the overview and in step 1, before either abbreviation is used. Step 1 has a worked example of the pip rule on a real pilot pip: the DEM cells along its recorded fall direction, with the 1, 3 and 5 cell tests drawn against the 0.7 m drop (0.99 m on the diagonal it falls along, where the 1-cell test passes by only 0.01 m). Clicking any other cell applies the same tests to it, showing why the cells either side are or are not pips. The rule's constants come from `instability_zones`, not from the page. The flow diagram no longer suggests that GNS SLIDE walls make pifs. Pips and pifs come from the DEM alone; GNS walls only flag the pifs they lie on and add `gns_only` candidates, and LINZ buildings only remove pifs. Numbered markers on those arrows, with a key below the diagram, now say so.
