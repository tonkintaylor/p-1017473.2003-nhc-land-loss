The loss viewer's summary calls the claims a cap leaves short "Claims capped", and "How the claims settle" counts a claim the cap leaves short as capped even where the excess then took it all, so such a claim counts under both and the rows can add up to more than 100%, as a note under the table says, giving how many claims, and what share, the baseline caps below their excess. The cap comparison table's tooltips say "claims capped" for the same count. A claim is capped wherever any of its repair is left unpaid, with no allowance of a dollar; a claim within a dollar of its baseline settlement still counts as unchanged. The scenario works out what it covers as the repair less what each limit leaves unpaid, so a claim no limit binds is covered for exactly its repair. The scenario's total settlement at the top of the summary, and its difference from the baseline, are shown to the nearest thousand, as the baseline's is; the land cap table keeps the nearest million. The note under "Damaged land structures" is shorter, leaving how walls are sized and bridges and culverts priced to the claim breakdown and the report. The note on how liquefaction was priced, and the note on undamaged and uninsured properties under "How the claims settle", are gone from the summary.
