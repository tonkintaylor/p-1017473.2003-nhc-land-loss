The claim is now the property rather than the address. The LINZ property
boundary sets the claim, every building standing on it is buffered by 8 m
including appurtenant structures, the address points inside it count the
dwellings that the per-dwelling sub-caps multiply, and the extent is clipped to
the boundary. A building straddling a boundary by more than 5 square metres and
10% is split and counted in both properties; a smaller overhang is dropped as
boundary error. Over the Wellington pilot this takes coverage from 4,764 of
8,591 addresses to 8,438 of 8,591 dwellings, on 4,388 claims.
