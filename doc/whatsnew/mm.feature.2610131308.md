The retaining wall type fragility table holds one curve per wall type, for a wall under 2 m. Walls 2 m and over now take both percentiles times 0.85 for every type (`TALL_HEIGHT_FACTOR`), and are no longer stored as separate rows. Fill and cut stay as factors on the same curve. The curves are re-set by judgement so they get flatter as they get stronger, weakest to strongest: garden timber, on the no-wall zone 5 curve (p15 0.30, p50 0.60 g site PGA), then brick or rock, crib or gabion, timber pole before July 1992, concrete block, reinforced concrete before July 1992, timber pole from July 1992, and reinforced concrete from July 1992 (p15 1.36, p50 5.12 g), spaced evenly in log PGA. Both the urban slope model and the shaking retaining wall damage state read these curves. The urban slope fragility curve figure in landslide step 5 marks p15 and p50, lists every curve weakest to strongest, and pairs each wall type's two height classes in its demand panel. `fig_rw_type_fragility.py` is frozen, since it reads the table's earlier form.
