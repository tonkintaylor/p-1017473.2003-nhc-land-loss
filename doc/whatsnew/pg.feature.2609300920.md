**A damaged wall that came with a landslide is replaced at the size the slip
needs, never smaller than the wall that was there.** Where a claim has both a
damaged wall and landslide ground, the wall the slip would need is sized
exactly as for a property with no wall, and the replacement takes the larger
size class and the longer length of the two. Where a claim has several damaged
walls, only its largest by face area is compared, because the slip is one piece
of ground and wants one wall.

A concrete wall is replaced in concrete, and a timber pole wall takes the pile
its new height calls for. Undepreciated value stays on the wall as it was, so
the cap does not move; only the repair cost does.

Four of the pilot's five such claims are enlarged, adding \$122,563 of repair
and moving the settlement from \$13.56 m to \$13.68 m. The largest is a 208.8 m²
slip beside a medium concrete wall 9.6 m long: it is now replaced as a large
wall 24.4 m long, for \$130,534, and settles at \$179,147.

Step 1 writes the replacement's size, length, face area and rate
(`replacement_wall_*`) and flags `wall_enlarged_for_landslide`. The
walkthrough's Repair cost tab shows the replacement next to the existing wall,
and the viewer prices the repair off the replacement rather than the existing
wall. Both still agree with the module.
